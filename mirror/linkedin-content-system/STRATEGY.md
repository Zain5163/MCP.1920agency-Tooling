# LinkedIn strategy: Rana Zain Usman (personal profile)

Written 2026-10-01. Algorithm facts were checked on that date (sources at the
bottom). Re-check them every 90 days. LinkedIn changes ranking often and does
not publish its algorithm, so most "facts" below come from third-party studies
and are labelled by how sure we are.

---

## 1. Positioning

**One sentence:** I run a digital marketing agency in Pakistan, and I am building
the tool I wish we had: your own AI running your social posting, ads, SEO and
reporting, with an expert's playbook built in and a human approving every rupee.

What makes him credible, and what the feed should keep proving:

- **An operator, not a commentator.** He runs real client work at 1920 Agency
  (social media management, SEO, paid ads, websites). Every tactical post comes
  from something he did, saw or broke.
- **Building in public, honestly.** The project log is full of real, specific
  findings: a Meta rule the sandbox never showed, a tracking gap found in Ads
  Manager, approval figures that were wrong and got fixed. Being specific is the
  brand. Hype is not.
- **Safety-first AI.** Everyone is selling "AI runs your ads". His angle is that
  the AI proposes and code authorises: approval tokens, spend ceilings that fail
  closed, campaigns created paused. That is the line to own.

**Stay on three themes.** LinkedIn's ranking model (360Brew) reportedly reads the
post text against the author's profile and posting history and narrows reach for
off-topic posts (third-party reporting, see sources). Stay inside:

1. Paid ads and social media management
2. SEO and AI search
3. AI and automation for marketing teams

Founder story and build-in-public posts are always told through one of these
three. No generic motivation, no politics, no unrelated news.

**Profile work (the owner does this himself, before day 1):** headline and About
should name these three themes and say "building an AI marketing tool, more soon".
The Featured section is where the waitlist link goes later (see the CTA ladder).

## 4. Cadence: 2–3 posts a day, and the risk that comes with it

**Chosen cadence:**

| Day | Posts | Slots used |
|---|---|---|
| Mon–Thu | 3 | AM, MID, PM |
| Fri, Sat, Sun | 2 | MID, PM |

That is 18 posts a week.

### Slots (all times Pakistan, PKT = UTC+5)

| Slot | PKT | Gulf (UTC+4) | UK (BST to 25 Oct, then GMT) | Central EU (CEST to 25 Oct, then CET) | Who it reaches | Default pillar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AM** | 09:00 | 08:00 | 05:00 → 04:00 | 06:00 → 05:00 | Pakistan and Gulf at the start of the working day | P2 tactical, or P4 story |
| **MID** | 15:30 | 14:30 | 11:30 → 10:30 | 12:30 → 11:30 | All four regions at once. The best overlap slot | P1 build in public / teaser |
| **PM** | 22:00 | 21:00 | 18:00 → 17:00 | 19:00 → 18:00 | UK and EU late afternoon, Gulf and Pakistan evening scrolling | P2 or P3, with document carousels here |

The posts are 6.5 hours apart. Clocks in the UK and EU go back on 2026-10-25,
so from then on those regions see each slot an hour earlier local time. Keep
the slots in PKT and do not chase the change.

**Assumptions, stated plainly:**
- Studies agree that **Tue–Thu** are the strongest days. They disagree on the
  hour: Sprout Social reports 11:00–17:00, Buffer reports 15:00–20:00 local, and
  Buffer finds evenings stronger than in past years. We assume his audience
  splits roughly into Pakistan and Gulf versus UK and EU. MID is aimed at the
  overlap and PM at the UK/EU afternoon.
- We have **no data from his own profile yet**. After 14 days, compare
  impressions by slot in LinkedIn analytics and move the slots to what his
  audience actually does.

### The frequency risk (read this before committing to 3/day)

Several 2026 analyses report that **posting twice or more in one day reduces
reach per post** (one reports a median drop of over 40% per post) because a new
post interrupts the earlier one's test distribution. They recommend at least
4–8 hours between posts. One source claims the current system no longer
cannibalises, which is the minority view. **Unverified either way. LinkedIn has
said nothing official.**

How we handle it:
1. Posts are at least 6.5 hours apart.
2. Each slot uses a different pillar and, where possible, a different format, so
   the posts compete for different readers.
3. **A 14-day test with a kill rule:** if the median impressions of 3-post days
   come out more than 30% below 2-post days, drop AM and move to 2 a day. More
   posts that each reach fewer people are not worth it.
4. One unit of quality beats volume. If a slot has nothing real to say, it is
   skipped. Filler is not posted.

## 5. Formats (what LinkedIn rewards in 2026)

| Format | Evidence | Use it for | Can our publisher post it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Document / PDF carousel** | Every 2026 study puts it first for engagement and dwell time (AuthoredUp: +39% reach on 3M posts; Buffer and Dataslayer agree, with different figures) | Checklists, "5 rules", playbook excerpts. 1–2 a week, PM slot | **No.** The social-publisher LinkedIn path does text, image and video. Documents are posted by hand until that is built |
| **Text only** | Weaker on raw engagement, but cheap and native, and the hook does the work | Build in public, opinions, stories | Yes |
| **Text + single image** | Mid | Redacted screenshots of real errors and Ads Manager screens | Yes (image upload proven live 2026-09-26) |
| **Short native video (30–90 s, vertical)** | Second on engagement in most studies | 1 a week: him talking about one finding | Yes (video upload proven live 2026-09-26) |
| **Polls** | Mixed: one source says reach is down and engagement negligible, another says there is still a reach lift. LinkedIn's March 2026 update targeted overused formats | **At most one every two weeks**, and only with a genuine question whose answer we use | Not through our publisher |
| **Articles / newsletter** | Native, so reportedly no link penalty; slower burn | Later: a fortnightly long-form "build log" | No |

**Signals that matter (third-party consensus, not confirmed by LinkedIn):**
dwell time, saves, thoughtful comments and private shares outrank likes. Write
posts people stop and read: specific, structured, worth saving.

## 6. Hooks

The first ~140 characters show on mobile before "see more", and ~210 on
desktop. The hook must work inside 140.

Hook types that fit his voice (and are true):

1. **The specific failure.** "Meta refused my first live campaign with one sentence: …"
2. **The counter-intuitive rule.** "Your Meta ad can be ACTIVE and not running."
3. **The number, stated plainly.** "PKR 500 a day. That's what my first live test spends."
4. **The decision.** "I decided the AI never gets to approve its own spending."
5. **The question operators actually ask.** "Who approves the budget when an AI builds the campaign?"
6. **The 'I was wrong'.** "I spent four attempts guessing at a Meta error. Three questions answered it."

Never use: "🚨", "This changes everything", "Nobody is talking about…", "I'm
thrilled/humbled to announce", "game-changer", "revolutionary", "10x",
"secret", "hack", "Agree?", "Thoughts? 👇", or "Comment YES and I'll DM you".

## 7. CTA ladder

The ask grows as trust grows. Most posts need no CTA at all.

| Stage | Weeks | Ask | Example close |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **Follow** | 1–2 | Implicit, or a soft line at most once a day | "I'm writing up what I learn building this. Follow along if that's useful." |
| 2. **Converse** | 2–4 | A specific question the reader can answer from experience. Never "agree?" | "Media buyers: do you tick website events on traffic campaigns, or leave Meta's defaults?" |
| 3. **Raise a hand** | 4–5 (after the name is final) | "Message me" / "the link is in my Featured section" | "If you run ads for clients and want early access, message me 'early'." |
| 4. **Waitlist** | 5–6 | A waitlist link in the **Featured section** (and in the post only on reveal day, accepting the reach cost) | "The waitlist is in my Featured section." |

**Newsletter (from 2026-10-05).** The owner's weekly LinkedIn newsletter, *Before It Has a
Name*, runs alongside every stage (see `newsletter/NEWSLETTER.md`). **Once edition 1 is
published, every new post ends with ONE short subscribe line** from the five in that file,
rotated, never the same in two posts in a row, after the post's own ending, with no link
(people subscribe from the profile). Until edition 1 is live, no post mentions the newsletter.
Edition 1's status is the `status:` field in `newsletter/editions/edition-01.md`.

Not used: comment-to-unlock giveaways ("comment GUIDE"). That is the "comment to
agree" pattern LinkedIn said in March 2026 it is reducing the reach of.

## 8. Six-week launch arc (2026-10-02 to 2026-11-12)

**The arc is tied to milestones, not dates.** A week moves on only when its
real-world gate is met. The product cannot accept customers on Meta until Meta
App Review passes (decision 0005), and the name is not final. Announcing a date
we cannot keep costs more than a slow reveal.

| Week | Dates | Phase | Story | Gate to move on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 Oct – 8 Oct | **Tease** | "I'm building something." The problem (AI + marketing without guardrails), the first live campaign, a real rule found | — |
| 2 | 9 Oct – 15 Oct | **Show the thinking** | Approval tokens, the spend ceiling, "AI writes, server knows, code enforces", the first campaign's real results | Campaign ended 7 Oct and results read from Meta |
| 3 | 16 Oct – 22 Oct | **Invite in** | What it does across platforms, what it deliberately won't do, genuine questions to the audience about their workflows | — |
| 4 | 23 Oct – 29 Oct | **Reveal the name** | Name, one-line promise, a short demo video | **The owner has finalised the name** (and ideally the domain) |
| 5 | 30 Oct – 5 Nov | **Waitlist** | Featured-section waitlist, who it is for first (agencies), what early users get | A waitlist page exists and works |
| 6 | 6 Nov – 12 Nov | **Launch / early access** | Early access opens, walkthroughs, first users' real feedback | The product can onboard someone outside 1920 Agency |

If a gate is not met, the week repeats its phase with new real material. Keep
building in public and do not rush the reveal.

## 9. Rules (non-negotiable)

1. **No invented results, numbers, client names, logos or testimonials.** Every
   number comes from the project log, a platform readback or the owner. Each
   draft's frontmatter lists its sources.
2. **Never name the product.** Until the owner finalises the name it is "what
   I'm building" or "the tool". Do not use the working name.
3. **Do not overclaim status.** Built, tested, live and available are different
   things. Only Meta can launch campaigns. Google, TikTok and the others have
   playbooks only. Nobody outside 1920 Agency can use it yet.
4. **Never name client accounts.** The ad token reaches client accounts. None
   of them are ever mentioned.
5. **Redact screenshots:** account IDs, campaign IDs, pixel IDs, token
   fragments, client names.
6. **No engagement bait.** No "agree?", "comment YES", like-chains, pods,
   reciprocal-engagement groups or comment automation (LinkedIn Professional
   Community Policies, and the March 2026 enforcement update).
7. **External links:** none in the post body by default. Studies report 45–60%
   less reach for link posts, and say "link in first comment" is now detected
   too. A LinkedIn product director has said publicly that links are not
   intentionally penalised if the post is valuable on its own. **This conflicts
   between sources and is unverified.** Policy: no links in posts; links live in
   the Featured section. Exception: the reveal or launch post, where a link is
   worth some reach.
8. **0–2 emojis, 0–3 hashtags.** One source reports more than 3 hashtags
   correlates with ~70% lower reach. Hashtags at the end, never mid-sentence.
9. **No competitor bashing by name.** Explain our design choice on its own merits.
10. **Not AI-sounding.** No "In today's fast-paced digital landscape", no "Let's
    dive in", no "Here's the thing:", no "–" lists of three empty adjectives.
    Generic AI text is reportedly suppressed. Every post needs one detail only he
    could know.
11. **Nothing is posted without his approval.** Drafts are drafts. See
    AUTOMATION-SPEC.md.

## 10. Voice

First person. Short sentences. Plain words. Confident without selling. Says
what went wrong. Uses the real nouns (ad set, pixel, approval token, PKR 500).
Explains jargon once for the founder audience. Ends with a point, not a slogan.
