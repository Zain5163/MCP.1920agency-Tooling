If you're bringing AI into your marketing team, don't start where the demos start.

The impressive demo is an AI launching a campaign from one sentence. In a real team, that's the last step, not the first.

The order I'd follow, and what each step should prove before you move on:

1. Read. Let it pull reports, summarise comments, go through an ad account's settings. Nothing changes anywhere. This step shows whether it actually understands your accounts or only sounds like it does.

2. Draft. Posts, ad copy, client updates, written somewhere a person picks them up, edits them and sends them. This step shows how often it's wrong, and in which direction.

3. Prepare. It builds the real thing, but in a state that does nothing yet: a campaign created paused, a post waiting for a slot nobody has confirmed. A person checks what was actually built, not the AI's description of it.

4. Act, with approval. It can publish or spend, but only after a person has said yes to that exact post or that exact budget. Not a standing "go ahead".

Most of what I'm building lives between steps 3 and 4. Every campaign is created paused, and nothing spends until a person approves the plan and the cost.

The mistake I'd avoid is drifting up the steps because the tool allows it. Moving from 2 to 3 should be a decision someone makes, based on what steps 1 and 2 showed.

Marketing leads and agency owners: which step is AI at in your team today, and what would it take to move it up one?
