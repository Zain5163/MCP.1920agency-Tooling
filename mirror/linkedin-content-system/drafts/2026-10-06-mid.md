I've written playbooks for 11 ad platforms. Right now, only one of them can launch a campaign from the tool I'm building.

Meta, Google, TikTok, Microsoft, Snapchat, Pinterest, LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Amazon, Telegram.

Each one has a section per goal (leads, sales, traffic, awareness and so on), its sources, and an "unverified" mark wherever the platform's own pages wouldn't confirm a fact.

So why write playbooks for platforms that can't launch yet?

Because I made a rule: no platform gets connected without its playbook. The expertise is written with the integration, not added afterwards.

And every playbook other than Meta says near the top that it can't launch from here yet. An AI can plan a Google or TikTok campaign and write the ads, but it is never allowed to claim a campaign exists when it doesn't.

A few things the research turned up:
→ several facts in my own earlier notes were wrong and got corrected (Google responsive search ads take 3–15 headlines and 2–4 descriptions, not exactly 15 and 4)
→ platforms change constantly, so each playbook carries a date, and if it's more than 90 days old the AI is told to check the platform's documentation first

Meta is live. Google is next.
