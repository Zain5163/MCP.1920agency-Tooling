The approval screen said "roughly 150,000 per month".

The campaign was set to run for two weeks.

That was a bug in what I'm building, caught in testing. It's also a mistake I see in real media plans all the time, so it's worth explaining.

A daily budget times 30 is the right number only for a campaign with no end date. If it ends in 14 days, the real figure is about 14 days of spend: roughly 70,000, not 150,000.

Either way, the person approving is looking at the wrong number. Too high and a good campaign gets turned down. Too low and someone gets a surprise invoice.

I fixed it in one place and found the same mistake in another: the activation summary was still saying "roughly PKR 15,000 per month" for a campaign ending in seven days. It now reads the real end dates and states the total from the moment of activation.

There was a second wrong line too: every approval said "This is public and cannot be undone." That's true of publishing a post. It isn't true of a paused campaign. Each action now gets its own accurate sentence.

The rule I took from it: the number someone approves has to be the real one, and so does the warning.

If you send clients budget approvals, check you're showing the total for the actual run, not a monthly figure out of habit.
