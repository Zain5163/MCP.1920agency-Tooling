An AI drafts my LinkedIn posts. It has no way to publish them.

It's the same rule I'm building into the tool, applied to my own feed.

Every morning a scheduled job reads my content plan, what has already gone out, and my build notes, and drafts the posts for two days ahead.

What it can't do:
→ post, schedule or touch my LinkedIn account. It isn't given a tool that can.
→ keep writing when I fall behind. If six or more drafts are waiting for me, it writes nothing. It held on its very first morning, because I had 18 unread.
→ make up a number. Every draft has to say where each fact came from. That rule is an instruction, not code, so it's the part I check hardest.

What only I can do: read the draft, change it, and type yes. Only then is it queued for its slot.

After that the text is locked. If I change my mind, I cancel the post and approve a new version. Nothing gets edited quietly once it's queued.

One post in the plan is about why I started building this. It has a gap marked for something only I can say, and the approval step won't queue it until I've written that part myself.

The test I use for any AI in a marketing workflow: if it got something badly wrong tomorrow, how far would the mistake get before a person saw it?

For my LinkedIn, the answer is a bad draft sitting in a folder.

For an AI with access to a client's ad account, the answer has to be just as boring.
