A link click is not a visit. On a Meta traffic campaign, that difference decides who Meta goes looking for.

When you set up a traffic campaign, the ad set asks what to optimise for. Two of the options look almost the same: link clicks and landing page views.

A link click counts the tap. Whether your page ever loaded doesn't matter.

A landing page view counts only when someone clicked and the page actually loaded.

So an accidental tap, or someone who gives up while a slow page is still loading, is a link click but not a landing page view. That's why link clicks almost always come out higher.

And Meta's delivery goes after whatever you ask for. Ask for link clicks and it finds people who tap. Ask for landing page views and it finds people who tap and stay long enough for the page to load.

If the job of the campaign is getting people onto your website, landing page views is the one I'd choose.

Before you launch, check three things:

1. The performance goal in the ad set. Look at it. Don't accept whatever was preselected.
2. The pixel on the landing page. Meta can now optimise for landing page views without one, using estimates, but with a pixel the views are measured, and you can see what people did next.
3. The page speed on mobile data. A click that never becomes a page view still cost you money.

Earlier this week I wrote about a tracking box I found unticked on my own live ad. This is the same lesson from the other side: Meta optimises for what you tell it to count, so count the thing you actually want.
